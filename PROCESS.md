# Process overview

MoveOut helps students discover free items and arrange collection before moving out. This account describes the implementation through [c7fe3ce](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/c7fe3cedf9205bc765610d01f5b2758d391f3cec) and verification through 8 November 2026. It was edited with AI assistance from my instructions, reported experience, repository history and verification results. Automated browser checks are distinguished from human evaluation; no user trial is claimed.

## Choosing what good means

I explored ideas with ChatGPT, including shared-fridge coordination, but kept asking whether they addressed a practical problem. Moving out supplied a deadline and an observable outcome: an item collected. The pivot in [ab091d7](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/ab091d7c22f21d30ab02b9fca04817a2d30bdc6a) separated requesting an item from confirming its collection. I excluded payments, delivery and chat so the project could concentrate on reliable arrangements rather than becoming a general marketplace.

ANU's [Give It Forward project](https://mccuskerinstitute.anu.edu.au/project/give-it-forward-circular-giving-in-residential-halls/) grounds the waste problem, without establishing that scheduling causes it. [Sloan's small-group software argument](https://www.robinsloan.com/notes/home-cooked-app/) and [Harley's status-visibility guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/), added during documentation review after initial implementation, helped articulate the design rationale. My definition of good is a clear, recoverable handover with little avoidable coordination. These sources support that direction; they do not prove MoveOut improves anyone's experience.

## Stack and agent workflow

The scaffold in [9cda16a](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/9cda16a3293dbdca1ff3136df34d8ee65a4c18f0) uses Node, Hono and SQLite. Built-in SQLite avoids another database service, while server-rendered forms keep core actions usable without JavaScript. Records and photos live under DATA_DIR on Fly's persistent volume. This fits one small server, at the cost of synchronous database work, responsibility for migrations, and an in-memory notification system that assumes one process.

Claude implemented changes; Codex reviewed them, and I returned concrete findings for correction. My experience of an agent reporting completed features and images that did not meet my expectations made completion claims insufficient. I asked for observable outcomes: another browser sees the update, an unfinished draft survives, and a rejected action changes nothing. The saved browser verification in [be44efe](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/be44efe09e169168a0f7f95a64da724ff7b3781c) supports this workflow, but agreement between agents is not independent evidence unless they actually exercise the relevant behaviour.

## Making shared state trustworthy

Persistence exposed an early assumption: the surviving Fridge Rescue database had an incompatible items table. [491a012](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/491a0129fbbdb9388df6a8f60d851576731f3c97) gave MoveOut a separate database filename without deleting the old file. That was a product pivot; subsequent MoveOut changes preserve existing records through migrations rather than starting with an empty database.

Real-time work in [a0f969a](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/a0f969aef015c1e773882e95fff596966cf2db63) uses SSE to signal changes, followed by a permission-checked page fetch. [ADR 002](docs/decisions/002-live-updates-preserve-input.md) explains protecting personal input while updating shared facts. A stale confirmation could reserve an item after its application had been cancelled; [ceb556a](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/ceb556a04f18a8e90448f1493eaf8b6ef79ff8c9) added regression coverage for rejecting that action without leaving a partial reservation. Business-action logging in [d9661d3](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/d9661d3e920946a9f1c1fdb9cef619815c7af10b) made accepted and rejected actions inspectable without recording raw identity cookies or private text; [ADR 003](docs/decisions/003-business-logging.md) records the trade-off.

Discovery widened the original link-sharing scope. The migration in [be9dd27](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/be9dd274781a11d19b0855c6f9fc99a5d016c5ea) made existing pages Unlisted, preserving earlier expectations. [ADR 004](docs/decisions/004-public-browse-visibility.md) explains the explicit visibility flag and database-filtered public pool. Public discovery helps recipients find items, while exact pickup details remain restricted.

## Pickup coordination and account ownership

I requested practical collection features: the agreed time and location, travel status, and a reschedule accepted by the other party. [beb41e7](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/beb41e7ee8f8f433c60edf323f05bb6d204df367) implemented them. Review then found that protecting an entire pickup card also delayed shared updates, and that filtering out the current time could leave an empty selector. [f753662](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/f753662f4bd3c6ec50fe1c3c22e253e5ce09f24d) separated shared facts from the protected form area and checked the actual alternative times. Its browser script exercises updates arriving during editing.

I next wanted people to reach their items and requests from another device. [74d57f6](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/74d57f6b34636bc6f17f0a6414995a587aab1593) added email/password accounts and association of existing browser-owned data. Keeping device access alongside account access appeared convenient, but it let the original browser manage account data after logout. [f19fc96](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/f19fc961b724a0f092cbc0f57cda2b82a7c95b86) changed the rule: an account-bound record requires that account's live session; an unbound legacy record is still recognised by its original device. Read queries and action guards must apply the same ownership rule.

Further review showed ownership alone did not enforce login on legacy writes. It also exposed stale private pages after logout, duplicate requests from one account across devices, and missing account-wide notifications. [b8f5970](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/b8f597045ed5441a0be0db5dd81a0e5a8c5338fe) added the shared login gate, permission-loss handling, account-scoped uniqueness and account notifications. One test had accepted a second device's duplicate request as correct behaviour. Correcting that expectation mattered as much as changing the implementation: a passing assertion can preserve the wrong requirement.

## Migration and recovery

The new uniqueness rule exposed two failures. Startup still recreated the old device-only index before removing it, so valid requests from different accounts on one device could prevent the server restarting. Linking an old anonymous request could also collide with the account's existing request and turn a correct-password login into HTTP 500.

[c7fe3ce](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/c7fe3cedf9205bc765610d01f5b2758d391f3cec) removed the obsolete index creation from startup and isolated individual association conflicts with savepoints. Conflicting requests remain unchanged; the signed-in user receives an explanation and a link to resolve the duplicate, then can retry association. Unexpected database errors still roll back the overall transaction. Restart tests retain the same data directory across real server processes; conflict tests preserve existing bookings and check that login succeeds. These failures extended the harness from ordinary interactions to the data shapes upgrades must survive.

## Verification and limits

The [CI run for 8beecd3](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/actions/runs/42371701314) passed all 88 tests, typechecking, evidence checking and deployment. Codex's earlier independent checks included the full suite and 24 conflict-flow browser checks at 1920×1080 and 390×844. Production checks also confirmed the published README matched the repository.

On 8 November, I requested end-to-end production timing rather than relying on SSE arrival. The script and [measurement record](docs/verification/realtime-2026-11-08.json) in [eb1ad49](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-tavewang/commit/eb1ad49703e3cba778c8a2b46022d6e6ca44404c) cover applying, confirmation, departure, proposing and accepting reschedules, and cross-device lists. Independent Chrome contexts exchanged desktop/mobile roles, with three repetitions per direction. All 36 samples completed without receiver navigation: 62–162 ms, median 77 ms. Timing starts at submission and ends after the receiving DOM changes and two animation frames; it approximates rendering, not physical screen response. These warm-server measurements do not establish weak-network or load performance. Earlier script-navigation and scrolling failures are disclosed in the record.

The same run prepared six Unlisted pages, six confirmed requests, two uploaded PNGs and two pending reschedules. Credentials, cookies and baseline details remain in gitignored local storage, outside the public evidence. A follow-up is scheduled for 9 November to compare saved arrangements and image hashes, log in from fresh browsers, and accept the retained proposals using original sessions. That check is pending, not a persistence success claim; even a successful return would not by itself prove a production process restart.

No human trial or measured coordination improvement is established. Password recovery and email notifications remain absent. My responsibility is to keep claims within the evidence and revise the acceptance criteria when users reveal what tests miss.
